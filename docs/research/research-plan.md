# Research plan

Last reviewed 2026-11-05. Part of the [research bundle](README.md). Requirement R74.

## Why we're doing research

The catalogue was built from public data, with checks on every figure, but without testing it with the people it is for. Research tells us whether it helps them, what to fix first, and where it should go next. Everything we learn goes into the [backlog and roadmap](https://cyberdudeuk.github.io/github-repos-asset-catalogue/#shape), in the open.

### Tactical: the next few months

| Id | What we need to know | How we'll find out |
|---|---|---|
| T1 | Can people who are new to GitHub find what they came for, without help? | Usability tests with the five commonest tasks; time, success and where people hesitate. |
| T2 | Which questions bring people to the catalogue, and which views answer them? | Interviews and the survey; compare with the role-based questions on Home. |
| T3 | Do people understand and trust the evidence labels, such as 'part of a live service', and their measured accuracy? | Usability tests: ask people to explain a label in their own words, and whether they'd act on it. |
| T4 | What stops people who use assistive technology from using the catalogue? | Sessions with screen reader, magnification, voice and switch users; fixes go straight into the backlog. |
| T5 | Which handovers to the DAM capability playbooks and the DQ Commons help, and which confuse? | Cross-portal journey tests (the six journeys in the three-portal review). |

### Strategic: where the catalogue goes

| Id | What we need to know | How we'll find out |
|---|---|---|
| S1 | Who needs this catalogue to keep existing, and who should own and fund it? | Interviews with data leaders, architects and policy leads; the owner's decision on a home (roadmap RM-11). |
| S2 | Should it become a registered govukOS node and a source of data feeds for other services? | Interviews with the teams behind GTI, ANA, LIAH and the shared registers; test the data/xref.json feed. |
| S3 | How does it serve the government's strategy for data, including the forthcoming Strategic Data Roadmap? | Map findings to the roadmap for modern digital government; re-map when the Strategic Data Roadmap is published. |
| S4 | How fresh and complete does the data need to be for people to rely on it? | Survey and interviews; compare with what refreshing costs (roadmap RM-09 and RM-10). |
| S5 | Is the split between the open edition and the government edition right? | Interviews with government and public sector users, and with people outside government. |

The six personas the catalogue was designed for: Policy lead (P1), Data leader (P2), Service owner (P3), Engineering and security lead (P4), Repository owner moving from co-cddo (P5), Cross-government architect (P6). Minimums for each round of about 12 people:

| Welsh speakers | 1 |

## Ways to take part

| Id | Activity | Time | How | What happens |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| M1 | Conversation about your work | 45 minutes | Online, one to one | We ask about your work, how you find out what others in government have built, and what gets in the way. No preparation needed. |
| M2 | Try the catalogue | 45 minutes | Online, one to one, sharing your screen | You try four or five everyday tasks while thinking aloud. We're testing the catalogue, not you. |
| M3 | Accessibility session | 60 minutes | Online or in person, with your own set-up | You use the catalogue with your own set-up and tell us what works and what gets in the way. We pay for interpreters and support. |
| M4 | Five-minute survey | 5 minutes | A GitHub form, in your own time | Seven questions about what you use the catalogue for and what's missing. Only totals are published, and counts under 5 are hidden. |
| M5 | Journey across three portals | 60 minutes | Online, one to one | You follow a task that starts in one of the DAM playbooks, the DQ Commons or this catalogue and carries on in another. |
| M6 | Ideas and votes | Any time | GitHub, whenever you like | Suggest an idea, or add a thumbs-up to the ideas you want most. Votes help us decide what to do next. |

## Making it inclusive

- Sessions at different times of day, with breaks, and a phone option.
- Captions on video calls; a British Sign Language interpreter on request, paid for from the research budget.
- Materials sent in advance, in large print, easy read or Welsh on request.
- Your own device and set-up for accessibility sessions; we never ask you to install anything.
- Age, sex, ethnicity and disability are asked only if you choose to answer, and reported only as totals, never against a person.

## Looking after what people tell us

- Taking part is voluntary, and people can stop at any time without saying why.
- We record a session only with consent, keep recordings outside GitHub, and delete them within 30 days.
- Notes use session codes (R1-03), never names. Names and contact details stay with the person who arranged the session and are deleted when the round ends.
- Findings are published as themes and totals, in the repository. Counts under 5 are hidden.
- Anyone can withdraw what they said within 30 days, through the contact form or by replying to the person who invited them.
- Nothing about a participant is ever added to the catalogue's data.

## From findings to the backlog

Each finding becomes an issue labelled `research`, with the session codes as evidence and a severity (blocks, slows, annoys). It is then assessed like any idea: see [Backlog and prioritisation](backlog-and-prioritisation.md).
